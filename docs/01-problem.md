# 1. Problem

## Personal data is at risk

1. In this information age, we are all at risk of having our personal data infringed upon by tech companies and mega corporations.
2. Women who use health and wellness apps are particularly at risk of having their private and intimate data at the mercy of tech companies that may not always have users' best interests at heart.
3. Only a small percentage of women use these apps, with privacy concerns holding most of them back.

## It has already happened

| Source | What happened |
|---|---|
| FTC (2021) | Flo Health shared sensitive health data from millions of users of its period and ovulation tracker with marketing and analytics firms, including Facebook and Google. |
| FTC (2023) | BetterHelp was ordered to pay $7.8 million and banned from sharing consumers' health data for advertising, after allegedly revealing sensitive mental health data to third parties like Facebook and Snapchat. |
| Duke University | A review of 23 popular period and pregnancy tracking apps found 87% shared users' data with third parties such as advertising and analytics companies. |

## Gaps in existing solutions

| Gap | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Your data lives on their servers** | Policy changes, sales or breaches put it at risk. |
| **All-or-nothing data use** | Per-feature consent is rare. |
| **Mind and body kept apart** | Mental health apps and cycle trackers don't share data, so mood–cycle patterns stay hidden. |
| **Perimenopause is an afterthought** | Trackers are built around regular cycles and fertility. |
